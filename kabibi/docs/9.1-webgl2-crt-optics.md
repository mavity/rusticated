# 9.1. Naked WebGL2 CRT Renderer & Geometric Thick-Glass Optics

## 1. Reassessed Goals & Architectural Overview

The current CRT presentation in `kabibi/browser/super-demo.js` (specified in `kabibi/docs/9-flourish.md`) relies on `three@0.180.0` (`THREE.WebGLRenderer`, `THREE.MeshPhysicalMaterial`, `THREE.ShaderMaterial`, `THREE.Raycaster`) to render three overlapping 96×56 curved planes (`screenMesh`, `glassMesh`, `reflectionMesh`) followed by a two-pass separable Gaussian depth-of-field (DoF) post-process. While the pre-stage (off-screen `xterm.js` WebGL canvas capture, 2× upscale, sepia warm-tint, and scanlines) and post-stage (linear-light separable Gaussian DoF blur in sRGB `RGBA8` targets) work well, the middle stage has two limitations:

1. **Synthetic Streak & Bloom on a Flat-Minded Overlay:** `reflectionMaterial` draws a hardcoded UV-space Gaussian diagonal formula (`vUv.x + 0.34 * (1.0 - vUv.y)`) shifted by a scalar `lightOffset`. It treats the room light as a 2D decal rather than light passing through thick curved glass onto the curved matte phosphor screen.
2. **Three.js Pipeline Overhead & Opacity:** Pulling in full `three.module.js` (~600+ KB) for a two-target scene with a single parametric surface introduces scene-graph traversal, material/program abstraction overhead, `MeshPhysicalMaterial` uber-shader compilation, and triangle-loop raycasting (`THREE.Raycaster` + 10,752-triangle CPU distance scans on resize) while obscuring direct WebGL2 state and timer-query profiling on low-end mobile GPUs.

### Reassessed Architecture

We preserve the battle-tested boundaries at the start and end of the pipeline while replacing the middle stage and the rendering engine with a physical two-layer thick-glass optical model and a dual-source 3D lighting rig:

1. **Retained Stage 1 — Off-Screen Terminal Texture Capture:**
   - Keep `xterm.js` `WebglAddon(true)` capture, `calculateCaptureViewport`, 2× canvas upscale, `sepia(40%) saturate(1.05)` warm tint, and `applyScanlines()` exactly as implemented.
   - Upload the resulting 2D `textureCanvas` directly to a raw WebGL2 `TEXTURE_2D` (`SRGB8_ALPHA8`).
2. **New Stage 2A — Dual 3D Pendulum Light Rig (Vertical Ellipsoid Diffuser + Fresnel Accent Sphere):**
   - Model two coupled 3D light sources oscillating together on the harmonic pendulum cycle (`REFLECTION_PERIOD_MS`), swinging both horizontally ($X$) and significantly in depth ($Z$) so that when the rig swings to the **left** it comes close to the screen ($Z \approx Z_{\text{base}}$), and when it swings to the **right** it recedes visibly farther away ($Z \approx 2.6\text{–}3.0 \times Z_{\text{base}}$):
     1. **Primary Fuzzy Source $\mathcal{L}_{\text{ellipsoid}}$ (Vertically Elongated Ellipsoid / Window / Office Fixture):** A large, bright, fuzzy 3D ellipsoid elongated vertically (semi-axes $\mathbf{R}_E = (R_{Ex}, R_{Ey}, R_{Ez})$ with $R_{Ey} \gg R_{Ex}$), recreating the pronounced diagonal/vertical smudge across the curved matte phosphor screen via refracted sub-surface illumination and curvature density lensing.
     2. **Secondary Accent Source $\mathcal{L}_{\text{sphere}}$ (Crisper Fresnel Sphere):** Located roughly at the base light position with radius $R_S \approx 0.55$, less blurred/smudged and weighted much more strongly toward **outer-surface dielectric Fresnel reflection** off the curved glass faceplate, giving a distinct glossy 3D bulb highlight alongside the broad sub-surface ellipsoid smudge.
3. **New Stage 2B — Thick CRT Glass Tangent-Frame Refraction, Subtle Newtonian Dispersion & Outward Blurred Halation/Glint:**
   - Render the curved CRT assembly in a single raw WebGL2 scene pass (`crtProgram`) modeling leaded CRT faceplate glass ($\eta_G \approx 1.52$, thickness $d \approx 0.036$ world units):
     1. **Tangent-Frame Primary Refraction & Curvature Density (Jacobian):** 3D Snell's-law refraction of the view ray entering $S_{\text{outer}}$ measured relative to the local shell normal $-\mathbf{N}_{\text{out}}$ (tangent-plane projection $\mathbf{T}_{\parallel} = \mathbf{T} + \cos\theta_t \mathbf{N}_{\text{out}}$), preventing off-center camera coordinates from dragging the entire texture in one world direction.
     2. **Subtle Newtonian Spectral Dispersion (Chromatic Refraction):** Physical wavelength-dependent refractive index splitting ($\eta_R < \eta_G < \eta_B$, $\delta_{\text{disp}} \approx 0.028\text{–}0.065$) along the tangent refraction vector, keeping central text crisp while adding delicate prismatic fringing near the curved glass edges.
     3. **Outward Curvature-Directed Internal Glyph Reflection & Multi-Ring Blurred Halation:** Bright self-luminous terminal glyphs emitted from $S_{\text{inner}}$ reflect off the inner side of the curved outer glass $S_{\text{outer}}$ **against the line of sight / outward along the convex curvature** (so letters on the left reflect further to the left, and letters on the right reflect further to the right). Sampled with a wide 12-tap multi-ring Gaussian/disk kernel (radius $\sim 6\text{–}18$ texels) and modulated by grazing Fresnel and a light-catching glint factor so it appears as a soft, glowing thick-glass reflection rather than sharp double-vision ghosting.
4. **Retained Stage 3 — Depth-Aware Separable Gaussian DoF Blur:**
   - Keep the exact two-pass (horizontal + vertical) 13-tap Gaussian blur driven by camera-space distance from the lower-right focal point, reading `sceneColor` (`SRGB8_ALPHA8`) and `sceneDepth` (`DEPTH_COMPONENT24`) framebuffers and outputting tone-mapped sRGB to the default backbuffer.

## 2. Removing `THREE.js`: Naked WebGL2 Engine Design

### 2.1 Dependency & Bundle Cleanup

- Remove `THREE_ESM_URL` from `kabibi/browser/main.js` (`loadBrowserDependencies`) and remove the `THREE` requirement from `KabibiBrowserSuperDemo.start(options)`.
- `super-demo.js` becomes a zero-external-3D-dependency WebGL2 module (requiring only `@xterm/addon-webgl` for off-screen terminal rendering).
- Keep the software-renderer guard (`WEBGL_debug_renderer_info` rejecting `swiftshader|software rasterizer|llvmpipe`) and context-loss fallback (`webglcontextlost` listener on the display canvas in addition to xterm's addon context loss).

### 2.2 Explicit WebGL2 Resource Layout

All GPU objects are managed explicitly in `super-demo.js` and deterministically deleted in `disposeScene()`:

| Resource | WebGL2 Type & Format | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `screenTex` | `WebGLTexture` (`SRGB8_ALPHA8`, `LINEAR`) | Uploaded from `textureCanvas` via `gl.texSubImage2D` when `screenNeedsUpdate` is true |
| `sceneFbo` | `WebGLFramebuffer` | Off-screen target for Pass 1 (Curved CRT + Thick Glass + Geometric Reflection) |
| `sceneColorTex` | `WebGLTexture` (`SRGB8_ALPHA8`, `LINEAR`, `CLAMP_TO_EDGE`) | Color attachment 0 of `sceneFbo` |
| `sceneDepthTex` | `WebGLTexture` (`DEPTH_COMPONENT24`, `NEAREST`, `CLAMP_TO_EDGE`) | Depth attachment of `sceneFbo`, sampled by Pass 2 & Pass 3 DoF shaders |
| `blurFbo` | `WebGLFramebuffer` | Off-screen target for Pass 2 (Horizontal DoF Gaussian blur) |
| `blurColorTex` | `WebGLTexture` (`SRGB8_ALPHA8`, `LINEAR`, `CLAMP_TO_EDGE`) | Color attachment 0 of `blurFbo`, sampled by Pass 3 (Vertical DoF blur + ACES tone map) |
| `crtVao` / `crtVbo` / `crtIbo` | `WebGLVertexArrayObject` + `ARRAY_BUFFER` + `ELEMENT_ARRAY_BUFFER` | 96×56 unit grid `(u, v)` in $[-1, 1]^2$; scaled and curved either in-place or via vertex shader |
| `quadVao` | `WebGLVertexArrayObject` | Fullscreen triangle/quad for Pass 2 and Pass 3 post-processing |
| `crtProgram` | `WebGLProgram` (GLSL ES 3.00 `#version 300 es`) | Unified curved CRT + thick glass refraction + internal phosphor glint + contour reflection shader |
| `blurHProgram`, `blurVProgram` | `WebGLProgram` (GLSL ES 3.00 `#version 300 es`) | Separable 13-tap depth-of-field Gaussian blur shaders |

### 2.3 Minimal Inline Math & Analytical Geometry (Zero Per-Frame Allocations)

Replace `THREE.Matrix4`, `THREE.Vector3`, `THREE.Raycaster`, and `THREE.Triangle` with pre-allocated `Float32Array` math helpers:

1. **Camera & Projection Matrices:**
   - `mat4Perspective(out, fovRad, aspect, near, far)`
   - `mat4LookAt(out, eye, target, up)`
   - `mat4InvertPerspectiveView(out, ...)` for reconstructing view-space positions in the DoF shader.
2. **Analytical Ray-Paraboloid Pointer Intersection (Replacing `THREE.Raycaster`):**
   - The CRT surface in world space is a paraboloid:
     $$z(x, y) = -k_z \left( \left(\frac{2x}{W}\right)^2 + a_y \left(\frac{2y}{H}\right)^2 \right), \quad k_z = 0.24, \; a_y = 0.72$$
   - Substituting a pointer ray $\mathbf{P}(t) = \mathbf{E} + t\mathbf{D}$ into $z(x, y)$ yields a **single scalar quadratic equation** $A t^2 + B t + C = 0$ (or linear when `flat = true`).
   - Solving this quadratic directly in `screenCoordinates(event)` takes <20 floating-point operations, completely eliminating Three.js's 10,752-triangle mesh raycast on every `mousemove`.
3. **Direct Focal-Distance Bounds (Replacing the 5,376-Triangle Resize Loop):**
   - Currently, `resize()` iterates over all 5,376 triangles and calls `THREE.Triangle.closestPointToPoint` to find `nearestDistance` and `farthestDistance` to `focalPoint`.
   - On the smooth paraboloid surface, `farthestDistance` occurs at one of the 4 corners (specifically top-left `(-W/2, +H/2)`), and `nearestDistance` can be found in <15 evaluations via coarse-to-fine grid/Newton refinement around the lower-right corner `(+W/2, -H/2)`.

## 3. Dual Pendulum Light Rig: Vertically Elongated Ellipsoid Smudge & Fresnel Accent Sphere

### 3.1 Coupled 3D Pendulum Trajectory (Left-Close to Right-Far)

Retain the exported helper contract (`calculateReflectionOffset`, `calculateReflectionDepth`, `calculateLightPosition`) while mapping the coupled 3D light rig so that its depth oscillation is clearly visible across the swing:

$$\phi(t) = \frac{(t \bmod T)}{T} \cdot 2\pi$$

$$\Delta_{\text{offset}}(t) = 1.2 \sin(\phi(t)), \quad \Delta_{\text{depth}}(t) = 1 + 0.2\bigl(1 - \sin(\phi(t))\bigr)$$

When the light rig is on the **left** ($\Delta_{\text{offset}} < 0$), it comes to its near baseline distance ($Z \approx Z_{\text{base}} = 2.2$). As it swings to the **right** ($\Delta_{\text{offset}} > 0$), it recedes visibly farther away in depth ($Z$ increasing to $\sim 5.2\text{–}6.0$ world units):

$$s_{\text{right}}(t) = \text{clamp}\!\left(\frac{\Delta_{\text{offset}}(t) + 1.2}{2.4},\; 0.0,\; 1.0\right)$$

$$L_x(t) = L_{x0} + \Delta_{\text{offset}}(t) \cdot W, \quad L_y(t) = L_{y0}, \quad L_z(t) = Z_{\text{base}} \cdot \bigl(0.95 + 1.55 \, s_{\text{right}}(t)^2\bigr)$$

Both light sources move together on this trajectory:
1. **Primary Fuzzy Ellipsoid $\mathcal{L}_{\text{ellipsoid}}(t)$:** Centered at $\mathbf{L}_E(t) = \mathbf{L}(t) + (0.28, -0.15, 0.15)$ with vertically elongated semi-axes $\mathbf{R}_E = (0.78, 1.95, 0.85)$ (mimicking a tall office light bank or window).
2. **Secondary Fresnel Sphere $\mathcal{L}_{\text{sphere}}(t)$:** Centered at $\mathbf{L}_S(t) = \mathbf{L}(t)$ with spherical radius $R_S = 0.55$.

### 3.2 Primary Light Path — Vertically Elongated Ellipsoid Illuminating the Matte Inner Screen ($S_{\text{inner}}$)

The large vertical ellipsoid $\mathcal{L}_{\text{ellipsoid}}$ primarily **penetrates the thick glass and illuminates the curved matte phosphor layer $S_{\text{inner}}$**, recreating the broad, pronounced diagonal/vertical smudge across the screen:

1. **Ellipsoid Metric & Inward Refraction ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{ellipsoid}} \to S_{\text{outer}} \to S_{\text{inner}}$):**
   - Scale the light displacement vector by the inverse ellipsoid semi-axes $\mathbf{D}_E = (\mathbf{L}_E - \mathbf{P}_{\text{in}}) / \mathbf{R}_E$ so iso-intensity contours form a tall vertical/diagonal band tilted naturally by the screen's paraboloid curvature.
   - Refract the incident light ray through $S_{\text{outer}}$ via Snell's law ($\mathbf{L}_{\text{glass}} = -\text{refract}(-\hat{\mathbf{L}}, \mathbf{N}_{\text{out}}, 1/\eta_c)$) for each spectral channel $\eta_R, \eta_G, \eta_B$.
2. **Pronounced Matte + Anisotropic Satin Phosphor Response on $S_{\text{inner}}$:**
   - Combine a broad **wrapped Lambertian area-light pool** over the curved inner normal $\mathbf{N}_{\text{in}}$ with a wide **anisotropic vertical-ellipsoid satin smudge** along the curved phosphor contour, boosted to a bright, clearly visible luminance level that matches the presence of the original smudge while responding organically to 3D distance and curvature.
   - Give the smudge a very subtle cool daylight tint and a soft Gaussian-darkened crossbar about 35% down from the ellipsoid's top, suggesting a window mullion or paired office-light panels without a sharp edge.
3. **Refractive Density Change (Curvature Lensing / Jacobian Factor):**
   - Modulate the inner matte illumination by the local refractive ray-bundle compression $\rho_{\text{lens}}(u, v)$ across the curved meniscus glass, plus subtle Newtonian dispersion at the refracted smudge boundary.

### 3.3 Secondary Light Path — Less-Smudged Sphere with Strong Outer-Surface Fresnel ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{sphere}}$ on $S_{\text{outer}}$)

While the vertical ellipsoid creates the deep sub-surface matte smudge, the spherical source $\mathcal{L}_{\text{sphere}}$ (radius $R_S = 0.55$ at the base light position) has a tighter angular profile and much stronger **outer-surface dielectric Fresnel weight**:

$$F_{\text{sphere}}(\cos\theta_v) = F_0 + (1 - F_0)(1 - \cos\theta_v)^3$$

- Evaluated via the outer glass reflection ray $\mathbf{R}_{\text{out}} = \text{reflect}(\mathbf{I}, \mathbf{N}_{\text{out}})$ against the 3D sphere $\mathcal{L}_{\text{sphere}}(t)$ (plus a restrained sub-surface core), this sphere produces a distinct, crisper, glossy glass highlight that glides over the outer faceplate and noticeably shrinks and dims as the rig recedes into the distance on the right side of its swing.

## 4. Thick CRT Glass Refraction, Newtonian Spectral Dispersion & Outward Blurred Glyph Reflection

The middle stage is unified into `crtProgram` rendering the curved glass/screen geometry into `sceneFbo` (writing both `sceneColorTex` and hardware `gl_FragDepth` into `sceneDepthTex`).

### 4.1 Layered Thick-Glass Geometry

Instead of two separate translucent meshes (`glassMesh` and `reflectionMesh`) rendered over `screenMesh`, the CRT faceplate is modeled as a physical leaded-glass curved shell of reference refractive index $\eta_G = 1.520$ and normal thickness $d(u, v)$:

- **Outer Glass Surface $S_{\text{outer}}(u, v)$:** The rasterized vertex grid at $z_{\text{outer}}(u, v) = -k_z (u^2 + a_y v^2)$.
- **Inner Phosphor Surface $S_{\text{inner}}(u, v)$:** Displaced inward along the local shell normal $-\mathbf{N}_{\text{out}}(u, v)$ by thickness $d(u, v) = d_0 \bigl(1 + \beta (u^2 + a_y v^2)\bigr)$, where $d_0 = 0.036$ world units and $\beta = 0.24$.

### 4.2 Step 1 — Tangent-Frame View-Ray Refraction & Subtle Newtonian Spectral Dispersion

For each fragment on $S_{\text{outer}}$ with world position $\mathbf{P}_{\text{out}}$, outer unit normal $\mathbf{N}_{\text{out}}$, and incident unit view vector $\mathbf{I} = \text{normalize}(\mathbf{P}_{\text{out}} - \mathbf{E})$:

1. **Reference Green-Channel Refraction ($\text{air} \to \text{glass}$ at $\eta_G = 1.520$):**
   $$\mathbf{T}_{\text{glass}, G} = \text{refract}\!\left(\mathbf{I},\; \mathbf{N}_{\text{out}},\; \frac{1.0}{\eta_G}\right), \quad \cos\theta_t = \max(-\mathbf{T}_{\text{glass}, G} \cdot \mathbf{N}_{\text{out}},\; 0.25)$$
2. **Tangent-Plane Slab Offset (Eliminating World-Axis Parallax Bias):**
   Because $\mathbf{P}_{\text{in}}(u, v) = \mathbf{P}_{\text{out}}(u, v) - d(u, v)\mathbf{N}_{\text{out}}$, a ray traveling along $-\mathbf{N}_{\text{out}}$ hits the exact same $(u, v)$ coordinate ($\Delta\mathbf{uv} = \mathbf{0}$). The true lateral displacement in surface coordinates is the **tangent-plane deviation** of $\mathbf{T}_{\text{glass}, G}$ from $-\mathbf{N}_{\text{out}}$:
   $$\Delta\mathbf{P}_{\text{tangent}, G} = \bigl(\mathbf{T}_{\text{glass}, G} + \cos\theta_t\,\mathbf{N}_{\text{out}}\bigr) \cdot \frac{d(u, v)}{\cos\theta_t}$$
   $$\Delta\mathbf{uv}_G = \frac{\Delta\mathbf{P}_{\text{tangent}, G}.\text{xy}}{\mathbf{S}_{\text{screen}}}$$
3. **Subtle Newtonian Spectral Dispersion (Prismatic R/G/B Splitting):**
   - Derive the Red, Green, and Blue inner-surface sample coordinates from $\Delta\mathbf{uv}_G$ with a physically restrained dispersion factor $\delta_{\text{disp}} = 0.028 + 0.045(1 - (\mathbf{N}_{\text{out}} \cdot \mathbf{V})^2)$:
     $$\mathbf{uv}_R = \mathbf{uv}_{\text{outer}} + \Delta\mathbf{uv}_G \cdot (1.0 - \delta_{\text{disp}}), \quad \mathbf{uv}_G = \mathbf{uv}_{\text{outer}} + \Delta\mathbf{uv}_G, \quad \mathbf{uv}_B = \mathbf{uv}_{\text{outer}} + \Delta\mathbf{uv}_G \cdot (1.0 + 1.18\,\delta_{\text{disp}})$$

### 4.3 Step 2 — Outward Curvature-Directed Internal Glyph Reflection & Blurred Halation

Terminal glyphs on $S_{\text{inner}}$ are self-luminous phosphors. Light emitted from a bright letter travels outward to the curved outer glass boundary $S_{\text{outer}}$ and partially reflects back toward $S_{\text{inner}}$:

1. **Outward Reflection Direction (Against the Line of Sight / Along Convex Curvature):**
   - On the convex CRT shell, the outward normal $\mathbf{N}_{\text{out}}$ tilts away from the center ($\mathbf{N}_{\text{out}}.x < 0$ on the left, $\mathbf{N}_{\text{out}}.x > 0$ on the right), and the grazing internal reflection increases outward away from the line of sight.
   - Consequently, a letter on the **left** side of the screen reflects/glints further to the **left** (meaning a screen fragment at $u$ on the left samples bright glyph energy from $u_{\text{bounce}} > u$, i.e. inward toward the center/camera), and a letter on the **right** reflects further to the **right**:
     $$\Delta\mathbf{uv}_{\text{reflect}} = \frac{\bigl(1.85\,\mathbf{N}_{\text{out}}.\text{xy} - 0.85\,\Delta\mathbf{P}_{\text{tangent}, G}.\text{xy} / d(u, v)\bigr) \cdot d(u, v)}{\mathbf{S}_{\text{screen}}}$$
     $$\mathbf{uv}_{\text{bounce}} = \mathbf{uv}_G - \Delta\mathbf{uv}_{\text{reflect}}$$
   - Because $\mathbf{N}_{\text{out}}.x < 0$ on the left half of the screen, $-\Delta u_{\text{reflect}} > 0$, so $u_{\text{bounce}} > u_G$: a fragment to the **left** of a letter samples the letter to its right, making the reflected ghost/glint appear **to the left of the letter** (outward along the glass curvature, against the line of sight).
2. **Substantial 12-Tap Multi-Ring Halation Blur & Glint Modulation:**
   - Instead of a sharp 4-point tap at $1.6\text{ px}$ that creates a readable duplicate character, sample a **12-tap two-ring disk kernel** (inner ring at $\sim 4.5\text{ texels}$, outer ring at $\sim 10.5\text{ texels}$, scaled by $1 + 1.35\,r^2$) around $\mathbf{uv}_{\text{bounce}}$.
   - Modulate the blurred reflection by:
     - **Internal Fresnel & Curvature Glint:** $F_{\text{internal}}(\cos\theta_t)$ boosted where the glass curvature and light/view grazing angles catch the reflection, with a low base floor so flat central text has no distracting double edge while curved regions exhibit a soft, luminous, blurred glass reflection and glint.
3. **Muted Newtonian Glyph Dispersion:** Add a small red/blue separation along the reflected-glyph direction, blended into the existing blurred reflection so the final composite blur keeps the rainbow glint soft rather than producing sharp chromatic fringes.

### 4.4 Step 3 — Full Optical Composition in Linear Light

At each fragment on $S_{\text{outer}}$, combine the physical layers in order from back (inner phosphor) to front (outer glass skin):

1. **Inner Phosphor Layer (Seen Through Thick Dispersive Glass):**
   - Dispersed terminal emission $\mathbf{C}_{\text{term}} = \bigl(T_R(\mathbf{uv}_R),\; T_G(\mathbf{uv}_G),\; T_B(\mathbf{uv}_B)\bigr)$ mixed with `screenBackground` on unlit pixels and the aperture grille.
   - Plus the **refracted matte room-light illumination** $\mathbf{C}_{\text{matte}}(\mathbf{uv}_R, \mathbf{uv}_G, \mathbf{uv}_B) \cdot \rho_{\text{lens}}(u, v)$ falling on the curved inner screen from the pendulum sphere $\mathcal{L}(t)$.
   - Plus the **internal thick-glass phosphor glint** $\mathbf{C}_{\text{glint}}$ from neighboring bright terminal letters.
   - Multiply this entire inner contribution by the outward glass transmittance $(1 - F_{\text{outer}}(\cos\theta_v))$ and subtle bulk glass tint $\exp(-\sigma_{\text{abs}} \, \text{pathLength})$.
2. **Outer Glass Surface Layer:**
   - Add the restrained **outer-surface Fresnel reflection** $F_{\text{outer}}(\cos\theta_v) \cdot \bigl(\mathbf{C}_{\text{ambient}} + \mathbf{C}_{\text{outer\_spec}}(\mathcal{L}(t))\bigr)$.
3. Write the composited color to `sceneColorTex` and hardware depth to `sceneDepthTex` for the subsequent two-pass DoF blur.

## 5. Retaining the Separable DoF Blur & Mouse Hit-Testing Parity

### 5.1 Two-Pass Separable Gaussian Blur (Unchanged Semantics)

- Pass 2 (`blurHProgram`) and Pass 3 (`blurVProgram`) keep the exact 13-tap Gaussian weights (`±6` taps, `maxBlurPixels = 8.0`), depth validity check (`step(depth, 0.999999)`), and squared proximity falloff:
  $$\text{proximity} = \text{clamp}\!\left(\frac{d_{\text{farthest}} - \|\mathbf{v}_{\text{view}} - \mathbf{f}_{\text{view}}\|}{d_{\text{farthest}} - d_{\text{nearest}}},\; 0.0,\; 1.0\right), \quad r = \text{proximity}^2 \cdot R_{\max}$$
- Because `crtProgram` writes the curved screen's depth into `sceneDepthTex`, both the refracted terminal glyphs, the internal thick-glass letter glint, and the geometric light-source reflection undergo the same coherent optical depth-of-field blur in the near lower-right corner.

### 5.2 Analytic Screen-Space Gasket

- Add a dark-grey, moderately narrow gasket in the final full-screen composite, not as 3D geometry or a separately positioned DOM/SVG overlay. Its contour is derived from the same projected paraboloid and current camera/viewport, then extended slightly over the rasterized silhouette to cover its jagged edge without changing the page padding.
- Use concentric rounded-rectangle distance fields for the gasket's inner and outer edges, centered on the rendered screen boundary so the gasket overlaps the edge equally on both sides. The width is 2.4% of the screen's shorter dimension. Each pair of corner arcs shares a center; their radii differ by the gasket width, preserving a rounded CRT outline rather than replacing it with an oval. Keep the corner radius small enough for the outer contour to cover the rendered screen's angular corner. Estimate each pixel's covered area from the continuous distance fields and their screen-space gradients. Fully covered pixels receive the gasket color; partial pixels blend that color with the blurred image in proportion to estimated coverage. This is a per-pixel coverage estimate, not MSAA or subpixel sample rendering.
- Evaluate the curved surface point and analytic normal at each covered pixel, then shade the plastic with a dark diffuse base and a rough specular response from the animated spherical light. The gasket is a shallow embossing relative to the terminal paraboloid: add a smooth raised cross-section centered on the screen boundary, displace the covered point by its profile height, and add the profile derivative to the paraboloid normal. Keep the profile height at 8% of gasket width so its extra slope remains subtle. Pass current camera, screen, and light values to the final pass each frame so the gasket remains aligned through resize and the highlight follows the animation.
- Scale the complete gasket illumination, including its light response, to 60% of the previous brightness.
- During screen power transitions, vary only the gasket's dark diffuse component with the broad screen-flare envelope. Keep the gasket geometry and specular response in place, and cap the response so the plastic remains dark grey rather than becoming white.

### 5.3 CRT Power Transitions

- After the first completed WebGL frame, leave the screen black for 800 ms with the bezel visible. Once the terminal host is ready and that delay has elapsed, run the 1200 ms power-on: grow a dim centre glow into a horizontal line; let its centre hotspot diminish as the line extends; expand the line vertically into a saturated white field; then reveal the current terminal image. Make the vertical expansion faster than the line growth, and fade the flare with a steep initial drop and a lingering tail as the image returns. Keep the effect inside the gasket's inner contour.
- On host exit or explicit `dispose()`, wait 500 ms, then compress the rendered image vertically into a centred horizontal line, then horizontally into a bright centre dot over about 800 ms. Fade only that dot over the following 1 second; do not replay the power-on glare.
- Keep the gasket fixed during both transitions. Apply the power effects and screen compression in the final screen-space pass, after the curved CRT image and blur have been rendered.

### 5.4 Refraction-Aware Pointer Mapping

- When the user clicks or drags on the canvas, `screenCoordinates(event)` intersects the pointer ray with the outer glass paraboloid $S_{\text{outer}}$ using the closed-form quadratic solver, then applies the same single-step Snell refraction offset $\Delta\mathbf{uv}_{\text{refract}}$ used by the shader before converting through `imageScale` to xterm client coordinates.
- This ensures mouse selection and click targets match the refracted glyph positions on screen to sub-pixel accuracy.

## 6. Profiling & Low-End Mobile Optimization Hooks

Because the renderer uses raw WebGL2 without Three.js wrapper state:

1. **Zero-Allocation Frame Loop:**
   - Pre-allocate all uniform vectors, matrices, and the 12–24 sample solver arrays once during `start()`.
   - When the terminal buffer has not changed (`screenNeedsUpdate === false`), skip `drawWarmTerminalImage`, `applyScanlines`, and `gl.texSubImage2D`, running only the 20-microsecond CPU pendulum contour update and the 3 WebGL2 draw calls.
2. **Optional GPU Timer Queries (`EXT_disjoint_timer_query_webgl2`):**
   - Support an opt-in `?profile_gpu=1` (or `options.profileGpu`) instrumentation hook that wraps Pass 1 (CRT + thick glass), Pass 2 (horizontal blur), and Pass 3 (vertical blur) in asynchronous `TIME_ELAPSED_EXT` queries—polling `QUERY_RESULT_AVAILABLE` on subsequent frames without ever calling `gl.finish()`—to report CPU solver vs. GPU pass timings on mobile devices.
