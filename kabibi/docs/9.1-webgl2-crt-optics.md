# 9.1. Naked WebGL2 CRT Renderer & Geometric Thick-Glass Optics

## 1. Reassessed Goals & Architectural Overview

The current CRT presentation in `kabibi/browser/super-demo.js` (specified in `kabibi/docs/9-flourish.md`) relies on `three@0.180.0` (`THREE.WebGLRenderer`, `THREE.MeshPhysicalMaterial`, `THREE.ShaderMaterial`, `THREE.Raycaster`) to render three overlapping 96×56 curved planes (`screenMesh`, `glassMesh`, `reflectionMesh`) followed by a two-pass separable Gaussian depth-of-field (DoF) post-process. While the pre-stage (off-screen `xterm.js` WebGL canvas capture, 2× upscale, sepia warm-tint, and scanlines) and post-stage (linear-light separable Gaussian DoF blur in sRGB `RGBA8` targets) work well, the middle stage has two limitations:

1. **Synthetic Streak & Bloom on a Flat-Minded Overlay:** `reflectionMaterial` draws a hardcoded UV-space Gaussian diagonal formula (`vUv.x + 0.34 * (1.0 - vUv.y)`) shifted by a scalar `lightOffset`. It treats the room light as a 2D decal rather than light passing through thick curved glass onto the curved matte phosphor screen.
2. **Three.js Pipeline Overhead & Opacity:** Pulling in full `three.module.js` (~600+ KB) for a two-target scene with a single parametric surface introduces scene-graph traversal, material/program abstraction overhead, `MeshPhysicalMaterial` uber-shader compilation, and triangle-loop raycasting (`THREE.Raycaster` + 10,752-triangle CPU distance scans on resize) while obscuring direct WebGL2 state and timer-query profiling on low-end mobile GPUs.

### Reassessed Architecture

We preserve the battle-tested boundaries at the start and end of the pipeline while replacing the middle stage and the rendering engine with a physical two-layer thick-glass optical model:

1. **Retained Stage 1 — Off-Screen Terminal Texture Capture:**
   - Keep `xterm.js` `WebglAddon(true)` capture, `calculateCaptureViewport`, 2× canvas upscale, `sepia(40%) saturate(1.05)` warm tint, and `applyScanlines()` exactly as implemented.
   - Upload the resulting 2D `textureCanvas` directly to a raw WebGL2 `TEXTURE_2D` (`SRGB8_ALPHA8`).
2. **New Stage 2A — 3D Pendulum Light Illuminating the Inner Matte Terminal Surface Through Thick Curved Glass:**
   - Model the 3D spherical light source $\mathcal{L}$ of radius $R_L$ whose center $\mathbf{L}(t)$ slowly pendulums left-and-right ($X$) and front-and-back ($Z$) on the existing 10-minute (`REFLECTION_PERIOD_MS = 600,000 ms`) harmonic cycle.
   - **Primary Path (Sub-Surface Matte Phosphor Illumination + Refractive Density Modulation):** Light from $\mathcal{L}(t)$ transmits through the curved outer glass surface $S_{\text{outer}}$ (attenuated by $1 - F_{\text{outer}}$), refracts through the thick glass slab, and illuminates the curved inner phosphor/terminal surface $S_{\text{inner}}$ as a **matte / satin reflection** (diffuse Lambertian + broad rough-phosphor lobe). When viewed from the camera through the curved glass, the refracted ray bundle compresses and stretches across the glass curvature (Jacobian flux-density modulation), naturally warping the perceived brightness and shape of the matte light pool. No secondary bounce of this soft room light back onto the terminal is needed.
   - **Secondary Path (Subtle Outer-Surface Fresnel Glint):** A restrained fraction $F_{\text{outer}}(\cos\theta_i)$ reflects directly off the outer air-glass interface $S_{\text{outer}}$, providing a subtle grazing-angle Fresnel sheen and soft outer-surface sky/bulb glint that separates the glossy outer faceplate from the matte phosphor bed beneath it.
3. **New Stage 2B — Thick CRT Glass Refraction, Newtonian Spectral Dispersion & Internal Phosphor Glint:**
   - Render the curved CRT assembly in a single raw WebGL2 scene pass (`crtProgram`) modeling leaded CRT faceplate glass ($\eta_G \approx 1.52$, thickness $d \approx 0.028\text{–}0.045$ world units):
     1. **Primary Refraction & Curvature Density (Jacobian):** 3D Snell's-law refraction of the view ray entering $S_{\text{outer}}$ and intersecting $S_{\text{inner}}$, together with the local refractive solid-angle compression factor that modulates light density across the curved glass.
     2. **Cheap Newtonian Spectral Dispersion (Chromatic Refraction):** Subtle wavelength-dependent refractive index splitting ($\eta_R < \eta_G < \eta_B$) along the radial refraction displacement vector, producing authentic prismatic rainbow fringing on high-contrast terminal glyphs and light gradients near the curved glass edges where refraction is steepest.
     3. **Internal Phosphor Reflection (Thick-Glass Glint / Halation):** Secondary bounce where bright self-luminous terminal glyphs emitted from $S_{\text{inner}}$ partially reflect off the inner side of the outer glass boundary $S_{\text{outer}}$ back onto neighboring phosphor coordinates.
4. **Retained Stage 3 — Depth-Aware Separable Gaussian DoF Blur:**
   - Keep the exact two-pass (horizontal + vertical) 13-tap Gaussian blur driven by camera-space distance from the lower-right focal point, reading `sceneColor` (`SRGB8_ALPHA8`) and `sceneDepth` (`DEPTH_COMPONENT24`) framebuffers and outputting tone-mapped sRGB to the default backbuffer.

## 2. Removing `THREE.js`: Naked WebGL2 Engine Design

### 2.1 Dependency & Bundle Cleanup

- Remove `THREE_ESM_URL` from `kabibi/browser/main.js` (`loadBrowserDependencies`) and remove the `THREE` requirement from `KabibiBrowserSuperDemo.start(options)`.
- `super-demo.js` becomes a zero-external-3D-dependency WebGL2 module (requiring only `@xterm/addon-webgl` for off-screen terminal rendering).
- Keep the software-renderer guard (`WEBGL_debug_renderer_info` rejecting `swiftshader|software rasterizer|llvmpipe`) and context-loss fallback (`webglcontextlost` listener on the display canvas in addition to xterm's addon context loss).

### 2.2 Explicit WebGL2 Resource Layout

All GPU objects are managed explicitly in `super-demo.js` and deterministically deleted in `disposeScene()`:

| Resource | WebGL2 Type & Format | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `screenTex` | `WebGLTexture` (`SRGB8_ALPHA8`, `LINEAR`) | Uploaded from `textureCanvas` via `gl.texSubImage2D` when `screenNeedsUpdate` is true |
| `sceneFbo` | `WebGLFramebuffer` | Off-screen target for Pass 1 (Curved CRT + Thick Glass + Geometric Reflection) |
| `sceneColorTex` | `WebGLTexture` (`SRGB8_ALPHA8`, `LINEAR`, `CLAMP_TO_EDGE`) | Color attachment 0 of `sceneFbo` |
| `sceneDepthTex` | `WebGLTexture` (`DEPTH_COMPONENT24`, `NEAREST`, `CLAMP_TO_EDGE`) | Depth attachment of `sceneFbo`, sampled by Pass 2 & Pass 3 DoF shaders |
| `blurFbo` | `WebGLFramebuffer` | Off-screen target for Pass 2 (Horizontal DoF Gaussian blur) |
| `blurColorTex` | `WebGLTexture` (`SRGB8_ALPHA8`, `LINEAR`, `CLAMP_TO_EDGE`) | Color attachment 0 of `blurFbo`, sampled by Pass 3 (Vertical DoF blur + ACES tone map) |
| `crtVao` / `crtVbo` / `crtIbo` | `WebGLVertexArrayObject` + `ARRAY_BUFFER` + `ELEMENT_ARRAY_BUFFER` | 96×56 unit grid `(u, v)` in $[-1, 1]^2$; scaled and curved either in-place or via vertex shader |
| `quadVao` | `WebGLVertexArrayObject` | Fullscreen triangle/quad for Pass 2 and Pass 3 post-processing |
| `crtProgram` | `WebGLProgram` (GLSL ES 3.00 `#version 300 es`) | Unified curved CRT + thick glass refraction + internal phosphor glint + contour reflection shader |
| `blurHProgram`, `blurVProgram` | `WebGLProgram` (GLSL ES 3.00 `#version 300 es`) | Separable 13-tap depth-of-field Gaussian blur shaders |

### 2.3 Minimal Inline Math & Analytical Geometry (Zero Per-Frame Allocations)

Replace `THREE.Matrix4`, `THREE.Vector3`, `THREE.Raycaster`, and `THREE.Triangle` with pre-allocated `Float32Array` math helpers:

1. **Camera & Projection Matrices:**
   - `mat4Perspective(out, fovRad, aspect, near, far)`
   - `mat4LookAt(out, eye, target, up)`
   - `mat4InvertPerspectiveView(out, ...)` for reconstructing view-space positions in the DoF shader.
2. **Analytical Ray-Paraboloid Pointer Intersection (Replacing `THREE.Raycaster`):**
   - The CRT surface in world space is a paraboloid:
     $$z(x, y) = -k_z \left( \left(\frac{2x}{W}\right)^2 + a_y \left(\frac{2y}{H}\right)^2 \right), \quad k_z = 0.24, \; a_y = 0.72$$
   - Substituting a pointer ray $\mathbf{P}(t) = \mathbf{E} + t\mathbf{D}$ into $z(x, y)$ yields a **single scalar quadratic equation** $A t^2 + B t + C = 0$ (or linear when `flat = true`).
   - Solving this quadratic directly in `screenCoordinates(event)` takes <20 floating-point operations, completely eliminating Three.js's 10,752-triangle mesh raycast on every `mousemove`.
3. **Direct Focal-Distance Bounds (Replacing the 5,376-Triangle Resize Loop):**
   - Currently, `resize()` iterates over all 5,376 triangles and calls `THREE.Triangle.closestPointToPoint` to find `nearestDistance` and `farthestDistance` to `focalPoint`.
   - On the smooth paraboloid surface, `farthestDistance` occurs at one of the 4 corners (specifically top-left `(-W/2, +H/2)`), and `nearestDistance` can be found in <15 evaluations via coarse-to-fine grid/Newton refinement around the lower-right corner `(+W/2, -H/2)`.

## 3. Pendulum Light Source: Inner Matte Illumination vs. Outer Fresnel Glint

### 3.1 3D Pendulum Light Trajectory

Retain the existing 10-minute harmonic period (`REFLECTION_PERIOD_MS = 10 * 60 * 1000`) and exported helper contract (`calculateReflectionOffset`, `calculateReflectionDepth`), mapping them to a physical 3D spherical light source $\mathcal{L}$ of radius $R_L$:

$$\phi(t) = \frac{(t \bmod T)}{T} \cdot 2\pi$$

$$\Delta_{\text{offset}}(t) = 1.2 \sin(\phi(t)), \quad \Delta_{\text{depth}}(t) = 1 + 0.2\bigl(1 - \sin(\phi(t))\bigr)$$

The sphere center $\mathbf{L}(t) = (L_x, L_y, L_z)$ swings smoothly across and in front of the screen:

$$L_x(t) = L_{x0} + \Delta_{\text{offset}}(t) \cdot W, \quad L_y(t) = L_{y0}, \quad L_z(t) = Z_{\text{base}} \cdot \Delta_{\text{depth}}(t)$$

with $L_{x0} = -1.7$, $L_{y0} = 1.8$, $Z_{\text{base}} = 2.2$, and physical diffuser radius $R_L \approx 0.55$.

### 3.2 Primary Light Path — Transmission Into Glass & Matte Illumination on the Curved Inner Screen ($S_{\text{inner}}$)

Unlike a mirror-only highlight on the outer surface, the room light primarily **penetrates the thick glass and illuminates the curved matte phosphor layer $S_{\text{inner}}$**, then refracts back out toward the viewer:

1. **Inward Transmission & Refraction ($\mathcal{L} \to S_{\text{outer}} \to S_{\text{inner}}$):**
   - A light ray from $\mathbf{L}(t)$ hitting the outer glass surface atincident cosine $\cos\theta_L = \max(\mathbf{N}_{\text{out}} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{L}}, 0)$ transmits a fraction $T_{\text{in}} = 1 - F_{\text{outer}}(\cos\theta_L)$ into the glass along the refracted light direction:
     $$\mathbf{L}_{\text{glass}} = -\text{refract}\!\left(-\hat{\mathbf{L}},\; \mathbf{N}_{\text{out}},\; \frac{1.0}{\eta}\right)$$
   - Because $\mathbf{L}_{\text{glass}}$ is bent toward the surface normal by Snell's law ($\eta \approx 1.52$), the light beam shifts and compresses as it crosses the thickness $d(u, v)$ onto the inner curved phosphor surface $S_{\text{inner}}$.
2. **Matte / Satin Phosphor Reflectance on $S_{\text{inner}}$:**
   - The inner CRT phosphor coating is a rough, powdery matte layer rather than a mirror. We evaluate a **matte + soft-satin BRDF** at the refracted inner surface point $\mathbf{P}_{\text{in}}$ with inner normal $\mathbf{N}_{\text{in}}$:
     - **Area-Light Diffuse (Wrap / Spherical Solid-Angle Lambertian):** Integrates the finite angular radius $\sin\alpha_L = R_L / \|\mathbf{L}(t) - \mathbf{P}_{\text{in}}\|$ of the spherical bulb over the curved inner normal $\mathbf{N}_{\text{in}}$:
       $$E_{\text{diffuse}}(\mathbf{P}_{\text{in}}) = \frac{R_L^2}{\|\mathbf{L}(t) - \mathbf{P}_{\text{in}}\|^2} \cdot \text{smoothstep}\!\left(-\sin\alpha_L,\; 1.0,\; \mathbf{N}_{\text{in}} \cdot \mathbf{L}_{\text{glass}}\right)$$
     - **Rough Matte Retro/Forward Lobe (Oren-Nayar / Wide Roughness $\sigma \approx 0.45$):** Adds the soft, chalky sheen characteristic of unlit CRT phosphor powder under oblique illumination, computed from the internal view ray $-\mathbf{T}_{\text{glass}}$ and internal light ray $\mathbf{L}_{\text{glass}}$.
3. **Refractive Density Change (Curvature Lensing / Jacobian Factor):**
   - As the viewer looks through the curved outer glass $S_{\text{outer}}$ at the illuminated inner screen $S_{\text{inner}}$, the thick meniscus-like glass slab (where thickness $d(u, v)$ increases toward the edges and corners) changes the apparent ray density:
     $$\rho_{\text{lens}}(u, v) = \frac{\cos\theta_{\text{in}}}{\max(\cos\theta_{\text{out}}, 0.15)} \cdot \left(1 + \gamma_{\text{curve}}\,\kappa(u, v)\,d(u, v)\right)$$
     where $\kappa(u, v)$ is the local mean curvature of the glass paraboloid and $\theta_{\text{out}}, \theta_{\text{in}}$ are the incident and refracted view angles.
   - This **refractive density modulation** compresses and intensifies the matte light gradient where the glass curves away from the camera, then smoothly attenuates it by the outward exit transmittance $T_{\text{out}} = 1 - F_{\text{inner}}(\cos\theta_t)$ as the light exits the glass to the eye.
   - As requested, no secondary bounce of this soft room light back onto the terminal is needed—the single inward transmission, matte phosphor scattering, and outward refractive density modulation capture the physical behavior directly.

### 3.3 Secondary Light Path — Restrained Outer-Surface Fresnel Glint ($S_{\text{outer}}$)

While the bulk of the soft light illuminates the matte inner screen through the glass, the outer air-glass boundary $S_{\text{outer}}$ still reflects a small fraction of incident light according to the dielectric Fresnel equation:

$$F_{\text{outer}}(\cos\theta_v) = F_0 + (1 - F_0)(1 - \cos\theta_v)^5, \quad F_0 = \left(\frac{\eta - 1}{\eta + 1}\right)^2 \approx 0.0426$$

- At normal incidence (center of the screen), $F_{\text{outer}}$ is only ~4.3%, so the outer reflection is barely perceptible and the sub-surface matte phosphor illumination dominates.
- Toward the curved periphery and the low-angle bottom-right corner where the viewing angle becomes more grazing ($\cos\theta_v$ decreases), $F_{\text{outer}}$ rises smoothly, producing:
  1. A subtle **outer-surface specular sheen** from the spherical light source $\mathcal{L}(t)$ (evaluated via the reflected view ray $\mathbf{R}_{\text{out}} = \text{reflect}(\mathbf{I}, \mathbf{N}_{\text{out}})$ against the angular radius of $\mathcal{L}(t)$), and
  2. A cool ambient glass rim tint (`vec3(0.78, 0.88, 1.0) * F_outer`) that visually separates the front surface of the thick glass from the recessed phosphor layer behind it.

## 4. Thick CRT Glass Refraction, Newtonian Spectral Dispersion & Internal Phosphor Glint

The middle stage is unified into `crtProgram` rendering the curved glass/screen geometry into `sceneFbo` (writing both `sceneColorTex` and hardware `gl_FragDepth` into `sceneDepthTex`).

### 4.1 Layered Thick-Glass Geometry

Instead of two separate translucent meshes (`glassMesh` and `reflectionMesh`) rendered over `screenMesh`, the CRT faceplate is modeled as a physical leaded-glass slab of reference refractive index $\eta_G = 1.520$ and thickness $d(u, v)$:

- **Outer Glass Surface $S_{\text{outer}}(u, v)$:** The rasterized vertex grid at $z_{\text{outer}}(u, v) = d_0 - k_z (u^2 + a_y v^2)$.
- **Inner Phosphor Surface $S_{\text{inner}}(u, v)$:** Located behind the outer glass at thickness $d(u, v) = d_0 \bigl(1 + \beta (u^2 + a_y v^2)\bigr)$, where $d_0 \approx 0.034$ world units and $\beta \approx 0.22$ models the thicker glass toward the CRT corners (where the outer faceplate is flatter than the inner phosphor curvature).

### 4.2 Step 1 — View-Ray Refraction & Cheap Newtonian Spectral Dispersion

For each fragment on $S_{\text{outer}}$ with world position $\mathbf{P}_{\text{out}}$, outer unit normal $\mathbf{N}_{\text{out}}$, and incident unit view vector $\mathbf{I} = \text{normalize}(\mathbf{P}_{\text{out}} - \mathbf{E})$:

1. **Reference Green-Channel Refraction ($\text{air} \to \text{glass}$ at $\eta_G = 1.520$):**
   $$\mathbf{T}_{\text{glass}, G} = \text{refract}\!\left(\mathbf{I},\; \mathbf{N}_{\text{out}},\; \frac{1.0}{\eta_G}\right)$$
2. **Trace Across Thickness $d(u, v)$ to Inner Phosphor Surface $S_{\text{inner}}$:**
   $$\Delta\mathbf{P}_G = \mathbf{T}_{\text{glass}, G} \cdot \frac{d(u, v)}{\max\!\left(-\mathbf{T}_{\text{glass}, G} \cdot \mathbf{N}_{\text{out}},\; 0.25\right)}$$
   This yields the base refracted UV displacement $\Delta\mathbf{uv}_G = \mathbf{uv}_{\text{inner}, G} - \mathbf{uv}_{\text{outer}}$.
3. **Newtonian Spectral Dispersion (Prismatic R/G/B Splitting):**
   - Leaded CRT glass has significant chromatic dispersion across the visible spectrum (Cauchy dispersion: $\eta_R \approx 1.508$, $\eta_G = 1.520$, $\eta_B \approx 1.536$).
   - Rather than computing three separate 3D `refract()` calls and intersections, we exploit the fact that the lateral tangential displacement $\Delta\mathbf{uv}$ scales monotonically with $(1 - 1/\eta)$ at oblique incidence. We derive the Red, Green, and Blue inner-surface sample coordinates directly from $\Delta\mathbf{uv}_G$:
     $$\mathbf{uv}_R = \mathbf{uv}_{\text{outer}} + \Delta\mathbf{uv}_G \cdot (1.0 - \delta_{\text{disp}}), \quad \mathbf{uv}_G = \mathbf{uv}_{\text{outer}} + \Delta\mathbf{uv}_G, \quad \mathbf{uv}_B = \mathbf{uv}_{\text{outer}} + \Delta\mathbf{uv}_G \cdot (1.0 + 1.25\,\delta_{\text{disp}})$$
     where $\delta_{\text{disp}} \approx 0.045$ is modulated by the grazing incidence factor $(1 - (\mathbf{N}_{\text{out}} \cdot (-\mathbf{I}))^2)$.
   - **Cost & Visual Result:** This requires only **two extra texture fetches** (`screenTex` R at $\mathbf{uv}_R$, G at $\mathbf{uv}_G$, B at $\mathbf{uv}_B$) and zero extra trig/sqrt operations. Near the screen center where $\Delta\mathbf{uv}_G \approx \mathbf{0}$, R, G, and B coincide and text is completely free of color fringing. Toward the curved glass edges and corners where refraction is strong, bright terminal characters and the refracted matte light boundary exhibit a subtle, authentic Newtonian rainbow separation.

### 4.3 Step 2 — Internal Reflection / Halation of Terminal Pixels in the Thick Glass

Terminal glyphs on $S_{\text{inner}}$ are self-luminous phosphors. Light emitted from a bright letter at $\mathbf{P}_{\text{in}}$ travels outward through the thick glass, hits the outer glass-air boundary $S_{\text{outer}}$, and partially reflects back toward $S_{\text{inner}}$, creating a faint secondary ghost/glint image offset along the curvature reflection vector:

1. **Specular Internal Bounce Offset:**
   - Given the refracted internal ray $\mathbf{T}_{\text{glass}, G}$, its internal reflection at $S_{\text{outer}}$ mirrors across $\mathbf{N}_{\text{out}}$:
     $$\mathbf{uv}_{\text{bounce}} = \mathbf{uv}_G + \Delta\mathbf{uv}_{\text{slab}}$$
     where $\Delta\mathbf{uv}_{\text{slab}}$ is proportional to $2 d(u, v)$ projected along the tangential component of $\mathbf{T}_{\text{glass}, G}$.
2. **Multi-Tap Internal Glint & Halation Sampling:**
   - Sample `screenTex` at $\mathbf{uv}_{\text{bounce}}$ (with a small 4-tap cross kernel scaled by glass thickness $d(u, v)$ and slight R/B spectral offset).
   - Extract the excess luminance above `terminalBackground` so only lit terminal glyphs/blocks produce internal glass reflection—dark background regions produce zero glint.
   - Weight by internal Fresnel reflectance $F_{\text{inner}}(\cos\theta_t)$ and glass absorption tint (`vec3(0.82, 0.90, 0.96)`), adding a subtle double-walled CRT glass ghost around high-contrast letters.

### 4.4 Step 3 — Full Optical Composition in Linear Light

At each fragment on $S_{\text{outer}}$, combine the physical layers in order from back (inner phosphor) to front (outer glass skin):

1. **Inner Phosphor Layer (Seen Through Thick Dispersive Glass):**
   - Dispersed terminal emission $\mathbf{C}_{\text{term}} = \bigl(T_R(\mathbf{uv}_R),\; T_G(\mathbf{uv}_G),\; T_B(\mathbf{uv}_B)\bigr)$ mixed with `screenBackground` on unlit pixels, aperture grille, and edge bezel line.
   - Plus the **refracted matte room-light illumination** $\mathbf{C}_{\text{matte}}(\mathbf{uv}_R, \mathbf{uv}_G, \mathbf{uv}_B) \cdot \rho_{\text{lens}}(u, v)$ falling on the curved inner screen from the pendulum sphere $\mathcal{L}(t)$.
   - Plus the **internal thick-glass phosphor glint** $\mathbf{C}_{\text{glint}}$ from neighboring bright terminal letters.
   - Multiply this entire inner contribution by the outward glass transmittance $(1 - F_{\text{outer}}(\cos\theta_v))$ and subtle bulk glass tint $\exp(-\sigma_{\text{abs}} \, \text{pathLength})$.
2. **Outer Glass Surface Layer:**
   - Add the restrained **outer-surface Fresnel reflection** $F_{\text{outer}}(\cos\theta_v) \cdot \bigl(\mathbf{C}_{\text{ambient}} + \mathbf{C}_{\text{outer\_spec}}(\mathcal{L}(t))\bigr)$.
3. Write the composited color to `sceneColorTex` and hardware depth to `sceneDepthTex` for the subsequent two-pass DoF blur.

## 5. Retaining the Separable DoF Blur & Mouse Hit-Testing Parity

### 5.1 Two-Pass Separable Gaussian Blur (Unchanged Semantics)

- Pass 2 (`blurHProgram`) and Pass 3 (`blurVProgram`) keep the exact 13-tap Gaussian weights (`±6` taps, `maxBlurPixels = 8.0`), depth validity check (`step(depth, 0.999999)`), and squared proximity falloff:
  $$\text{proximity} = \text{clamp}\!\left(\frac{d_{\text{farthest}} - \|\mathbf{v}_{\text{view}} - \mathbf{f}_{\text{view}}\|}{d_{\text{farthest}} - d_{\text{nearest}}},\; 0.0,\; 1.0\right), \quad r = \text{proximity}^2 \cdot R_{\max}$$
- Because `crtProgram` writes the curved screen's depth into `sceneDepthTex`, both the refracted terminal glyphs, the internal thick-glass letter glint, and the geometric light-source reflection undergo the same coherent optical depth-of-field blur in the near lower-right corner.

### 5.2 Refraction-Aware Pointer Mapping

- When the user clicks or drags on the canvas, `screenCoordinates(event)` intersects the pointer ray with the outer glass paraboloid $S_{\text{outer}}$ using the closed-form quadratic solver, then applies the same single-step Snell refraction offset $\Delta\mathbf{uv}_{\text{refract}}$ used by the shader before converting through `imageScale` to xterm client coordinates.
- This ensures mouse selection and click targets match the refracted glyph positions on screen to sub-pixel accuracy.

## 6. Profiling & Low-End Mobile Optimization Hooks

Because the renderer uses raw WebGL2 without Three.js wrapper state:

1. **Zero-Allocation Frame Loop:**
   - Pre-allocate all uniform vectors, matrices, and the 12–24 sample solver arrays once during `start()`.
   - When the terminal buffer has not changed (`screenNeedsUpdate === false`), skip `drawWarmTerminalImage`, `applyScanlines`, and `gl.texSubImage2D`, running only the 20-microsecond CPU pendulum contour update and the 3 WebGL2 draw calls.
2. **Optional GPU Timer Queries (`EXT_disjoint_timer_query_webgl2`):**
   - Support an opt-in `?profile_gpu=1` (or `options.profileGpu`) instrumentation hook that wraps Pass 1 (CRT + thick glass), Pass 2 (horizontal blur), and Pass 3 (vertical blur) in asynchronous `TIME_ELAPSED_EXT` queries—polling `QUERY_RESULT_AVAILABLE` on subsequent frames without ever calling `gl.finish()`—to report CPU solver vs. GPU pass timings on mobile devices.
