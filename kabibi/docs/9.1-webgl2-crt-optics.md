# Naked WebGL2 CRT Renderer & Geometric Thick-Glass Optics

## 1. Reassessed Goals & Architectural Overview

The current CRT presentation in `kabibi/browser/super-demo.js` relies on `three@0.180.0` (`THREE.WebGLRenderer`, `THREE.MeshPhysicalMaterial`, `THREE.ShaderMaterial`, `THREE.Raycaster`) to render three overlapping 96×56 curved planes (`screenMesh`, `glassMesh`, `reflectionMesh`) followed by a two-pass separable Gaussian depth-of-field (DoF) post-process. While the pre-stage (off-screen `xterm.js` WebGL canvas capture, 2× upscale, sepia warm-tint, and scanlines) and post-stage (linear-light separable Gaussian DoF blur in sRGB `RGBA8` targets) work well, the middle stage has two limitations:

1. **Synthetic Streak & Bloom:** `reflectionMaterial` draws a hardcoded UV-space Gaussian diagonal formula (`vUv.x + 0.34 * (1.0 - vUv.y)`) shifted by a scalar `lightOffset`. It is not derived from the 3D geometry of a light source reflecting off the curved glass surface.
2. **Three.js Pipeline Overhead & Opacity:** Pulling in full `three.module.js` (~600+ KB) for a two-target scene with a single parametric surface introduces scene-graph traversal, material/program abstraction overhead, `MeshPhysicalMaterial` uber-shader compilation, and triangle-loop raycasting (`THREE.Raycaster` + 10,752-triangle CPU distance scans on resize) while obscuring direct WebGL2 state and timer-query profiling on low-end mobile GPUs.

### Reassessed Architecture

We preserve the battle-tested boundaries at the start and end of the pipeline while replacing the middle stage and the rendering engine:

1. **Retained Stage 1 — Off-Screen Terminal Texture Capture:**
   - Keep `xterm.js` `WebglAddon(true)` capture, `calculateCaptureViewport`, 2× canvas upscale, `sepia(40%) saturate(1.05)` warm tint, and `applyScanlines()` exactly as implemented.
   - Upload the resulting 2D `textureCanvas` directly to a raw WebGL2 `TEXTURE_2D` (`SRGB8_ALPHA8`).
2. **New Stage 2A — CPU-Side Geometric Light-Source Contour Reflection Solver (12–24 Samples):**
   - Model an actual 3D spherical light source $\mathcal{L}$ of radius $R_L$ whose center $\mathbf{L}(t)$ slowly pendulums left-and-right ($X$) and front-and-back ($Z$) on the existing 10-minute (`REFLECTION_PERIOD_MS = 600,000 ms`) harmonic cycle.
   - Because the room light reflection is inherently a soft, out-of-focus smudge on the glass, per-pixel ray-sphere intersection in the fragment shader is wasted GPU work.
   - Instead, once per frame on the CPU, sample $N \in [12, 24]$ boundary and spine points on the spherical light source, project their exact specular reflection loci onto the curved outer glass surface $S_{\text{outer}}$ with respect to the camera eye $\mathbf{E}$, and fit a compact parametric reflection curve (spine control points, anisotropic cross-axis widths, and Fresnel/radiometric intensity weights). Pass these compact curve uniforms to the GPU.
3. **New Stage 2B — Fragment-Shader Thick CRT Glass Refraction & Internal Phosphor Glint:**
   - Unlike the blurry room light, the terminal characters sit directly behind a thick curved glass faceplate (classic CRT implosion-shield / leaded glass thickness $d \approx 0.028\text{–}0.042$ world units, refractive index $\eta \approx 1.52$).
   - Render the curved outer glass surface in a single raw WebGL2 scene pass that computes:
     1. **Primary Refraction:** 3D Snell's-law refraction of the view ray entering the curved outer glass surface $S_{\text{outer}}$ and intersecting the inner phosphor surface $S_{\text{inner}}$, naturally bending and magnifying terminal glyphs near the curved edges and bezel.
     2. **Internal Phosphor Reflection (Thick-Glass Glint / Halation):** Secondary bounce where bright terminal pixels emitted from $S_{\text{inner}}$ travel through the glass thickness, partially reflect off the outer glass-air boundary $S_{\text{outer}}$ via internal Fresnel reflectance, and land back on neighboring coordinates of $S_{\text{inner}}$. Because this glint depends on the live terminal pixel colors (bright text glyphs next to dark background), it is evaluated in the fragment shader via displaced multi-tap sampling of the terminal texture.
     3. **Geometric Light-Source Smudge Composite:** Smooth evaluation of the CPU-derived 3D reflection contour and Fresnel sheen over the outer glass surface.
4. **Retained Stage 3 — Depth-Aware Separable Gaussian DoF Blur:**
   - Keep the exact two-pass (horizontal + vertical) 13-tap Gaussian blur driven by camera-space distance from the lower-right focal point, reading `sceneColor` (`SRGB8_ALPHA8`) and `sceneDepth` (`DEPTH_COMPONENT24`) framebuffers and outputting tone-mapped sRGB to the default backbuffer.

## 2. Removing `THREE.js`: Naked WebGL2 Engine Design

### 2.1 Dependency & Bundle Cleanup

- Remove `THREE_ESM_URL` from `kabibi/browser/main.js` (`loadBrowserDependencies`) and remove the `THREE` requirement from `KabibiBrowserSuperDemo.start(options)`.
- `super-demo.js` becomes a zero-external-3D-dependency WebGL2 module (requiring only `@xterm/addon-webgl` for off-screen terminal rendering).
- Keep the software-renderer guard (`WEBGL_debug_renderer_info` rejecting `swiftshader|software rasterizer|llvmpipe`) and context-loss fallback (`webglcontextlost` listener on the display canvas in addition to xterm's addon context loss).

### 2.2 Explicit WebGL2 Resource Layout

All GPU objects are managed explicitly in `super-demo.js` and deterministically deleted in `disposeScene()`:

| Resource | WebGL2 Type & Format | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `screenTex` | `WebGLTexture` (`SRGB8_ALPHA8`, `LINEAR`) | Uploaded from `textureCanvas` via `gl.texSubImage2D` when `screenNeedsUpdate` is true |
| `sceneFbo` | `WebGLFramebuffer` | Off-screen target for Pass 1 (Curved CRT + Thick Glass + Geometric Reflection) |
| `sceneColorTex` | `WebGLTexture` (`SRGB8_ALPHA8`, `LINEAR`, `CLAMP_TO_EDGE`) | Color attachment 0 of `sceneFbo` |
| `sceneDepthTex` | `WebGLTexture` (`DEPTH_COMPONENT24`, `NEAREST`, `CLAMP_TO_EDGE`) | Depth attachment of `sceneFbo`, sampled by Pass 2 & Pass 3 DoF shaders |
| `blurFbo` | `WebGLFramebuffer` | Off-screen target for Pass 2 (Horizontal DoF Gaussian blur) |
| `blurColorTex` | `WebGLTexture` (`SRGB8_ALPHA8`, `LINEAR`, `CLAMP_TO_EDGE`) | Color attachment 0 of `blurFbo`, sampled by Pass 3 (Vertical DoF blur + ACES tone map) |
| `crtVao` / `crtVbo` / `crtIbo` | `WebGLVertexArrayObject` + `ARRAY_BUFFER` + `ELEMENT_ARRAY_BUFFER` | 96×56 unit grid `(u, v)` in $[-1, 1]^2$; scaled and curved either in-place or via vertex shader |
| `quadVao` | `WebGLVertexArrayObject` | Fullscreen triangle/quad for Pass 2 and Pass 3 post-processing |
| `crtProgram` | `WebGLProgram` (GLSL ES 3.00 `#version 300 es`) | Unified curved CRT + thick glass refraction + internal phosphor glint + contour reflection shader |
| `blurHProgram`, `blurVProgram` | `WebGLProgram` (GLSL ES 3.00 `#version 300 es`) | Separable 13-tap depth-of-field Gaussian blur shaders |

### 2.3 Minimal Inline Math & Analytical Geometry (Zero Per-Frame Allocations)

Replace `THREE.Matrix4`, `THREE.Vector3`, `THREE.Raycaster`, and `THREE.Triangle` with pre-allocated `Float32Array` math helpers:

1. **Camera & Projection Matrices:**
   - `mat4Perspective(out, fovRad, aspect, near, far)`
   - `mat4LookAt(out, eye, target, up)`
   - `mat4InvertPerspectiveView(out, ...)` for reconstructing view-space positions in the DoF shader.
2. **Analytical Ray-Paraboloid Pointer Intersection (Replacing `THREE.Raycaster`):**
   - The CRT surface in world space is a paraboloid:
     $$z(x, y) = -k_z \left( \left(\frac{2x}{W}\right)^2 + a_y \left(\frac{2y}{H}\right)^2 \right), \quad k_z = 0.24, \; a_y = 0.72$$
   - Substituting a pointer ray $\mathbf{P}(t) = \mathbf{E} + t\mathbf{D}$ into $z(x, y)$ yields a **single scalar quadratic equation** $A t^2 + B t + C = 0$ (or linear when `flat = true`).
   - Solving this quadratic directly in `screenCoordinates(event)` takes <20 floating-point operations, completely eliminating Three.js's 10,752-triangle mesh raycast on every `mousemove`.
3. **Direct Focal-Distance Bounds (Replacing the 5,376-Triangle Resize Loop):**
   - Currently, `resize()` iterates over all 5,376 triangles and calls `THREE.Triangle.closestPointToPoint` to find `nearestDistance` and `farthestDistance` to `focalPoint`.
   - On the smooth paraboloid surface, `farthestDistance` occurs at one of the 4 corners (specifically top-left `(-W/2, +H/2)`), and `nearestDistance` can be found in <15 evaluations via coarse-to-fine grid/Newton refinement around the lower-right corner `(+W/2, -H/2)`.

## 3. Pendulum Light Source & 12–24 Point Contour Reflection Geometry

### 3.1 3D Pendulum Light Trajectory

Retain the existing 10-minute harmonic period (`REFLECTION_PERIOD_MS = 10 * 60 * 1000`) and exported helper contract (`calculateReflectionOffset`, `calculateReflectionDepth`), mapping them to a physical 3D spherical light source $\mathcal{L}$ of radius $R_L$:

$$\phi(t) = \frac{(t \bmod T)}{T} \cdot 2\pi$$

$$\Delta_{\text{offset}}(t) = 1.2 \sin(\phi(t)), \quad \Delta_{\text{depth}}(t) = 1 + 0.2\bigl(1 - \sin(\phi(t))\bigr)$$

The sphere center $\mathbf{L}(t) = (L_x, L_y, L_z)$ swings smoothly across and in front of the screen:

$$L_x(t) = L_{x0} + \Delta_{\text{offset}}(t) \cdot W, \quad L_y(t) = L_{y0}, \quad L_z(t) = Z_{\text{base}} \cdot \Delta_{\text{depth}}(t)$$

with $L_{x0} = -1.7$, $L_{y0} = 1.8$, $Z_{\text{base}} = 2.2$, and physical bulb/diffuser radius $R_L \approx 0.55$.

### 3.2 CPU-Side Specular Reflection Solver for 12–24 Light Sample Points

Instead of per-pixel ray-sphere casting in the fragment shader, we compute the reflection geometry on the CPU once per frame in ~20 microseconds:

1. **Sample Point Generation ($N = 16$ or $24$ points):**
   - Construct an orthonormal basis $(\mathbf{u}_L, \mathbf{v}_L, \mathbf{w}_L)$ at the light center $\mathbf{L}(t)$ facing the screen center.
   - Place $N$ sample points $\mathbf{P}_i$ on the light source:
     - An outer ring of $M = 12\text{–}16$ boundary points around the silhouette disc of radius $R_L$:
       $$\mathbf{P}_k = \mathbf{L}(t) + R_L \bigl(\cos\theta_k\,\mathbf{u}_L + \sin\theta_k\,\mathbf{v}_L\bigr)$$
     - Plus $4\text{–}8$ interior/axis points along the horizontal and vertical diameters of the light sphere (including the front pole $\mathbf{L}(t) - R_L \mathbf{w}_L$).
2. **Reflection Point Projection onto the Outer Curved Glass $S_{\text{outer}}$:**
   - The outer glass surface has world equation:
     $$\mathbf{S}(u, v) = \left( u \frac{W}{2},\; v \frac{H}{2},\; z_{\text{glass}} - k_z (u^2 + a_y v^2) \right), \quad (u, v) \in [-1, 1]^2$$
     with unit normal $\mathbf{N}(u, v) = \text{normalize}\!\left(\frac{4 k_z u}{W},\; \frac{4 k_z a_y v}{H},\; 1\right)$.
   - For each light sample $\mathbf{P}_i$ and camera position $\mathbf{E}$, the specular reflection point $(u_i, v_i)$ on the glass is the stationary point of the optical path length $f(u, v) = \|\mathbf{P}_i - \mathbf{S}(u, v)\| + \|\mathbf{E} - \mathbf{S}(u, v)\|$ (equivalently, where the half-vector $\mathbf{H}_i = \text{normalize}(\hat{\mathbf{L}}_i + \hat{\mathbf{V}}_i)$ is parallel to $\mathbf{N}(u, v)$).
   - Because $k_z = 0.24$ is a gentle paraboloid, 2 to 3 iterations of 2×2 Newton-Raphson (seeded from the previous frame's $(u_i, v_i)$ or the flat-plane intersection) converge to machine precision (`< 1e-6`).
   - Note that $(u_i, v_i)$ is solved in **unclamped** surface coordinates: as the pendulum swings to the far left or right, the reflection locus can partially or fully slide off the $[-1, 1]^2$ screen edge naturally.
3. **Deriving the Parametric Reflection Smudge & Intensity Envelope:**
   - From the reflected UV coordinates $\{(u_i, v_i)\}$ on the glass surface:
     - **Reflected Spine / Quadratic Arc:** Fit 3 control points $(\mathbf{c}_0, \mathbf{c}_1, \mathbf{c}_2)$ in UV space spanning the reflected light axis, capturing the natural curvature of the highlight induced by the paraboloid's astigmatism ($a_y = 0.72 \neq 1$).
     - **Local Radiometric & Minor-Axis Widths $(w_0, w_1, w_2)$:** Measure the perpendicular spread of the reflected boundary points around the spine at each control point. Because the paraboloid acts as a convex mirror whose local principal curvatures vary across the screen, the reflected smudge naturally narrows or widens and foreshortens toward the screen edges.
     - **Physical Intensity & Fresnel Weights $(I_0, I_1, I_2)$:** At each spine point, compute:
       - Inverse-square / solid-angle falloff from $\|\mathbf{L}(t) - \mathbf{S}(u, v)\|^2$, so the reflection brightens when the pendulum swings closer in $Z$ and dims when it recedes.
       - Schlick Fresnel reflectance $F(\cos\theta_d) = F_0 + (1 - F_0)(1 - \cos\theta_d)^5$ with glass $F_0 = \left(\frac{\eta - 1}{\eta + 1}\right)^2 \approx 0.0426$ ($\eta = 1.52$).
   - Upload the fitted spine control points, widths, and intensities as compact `vec3`/`vec4` uniforms to `crtProgram`.

## 4. Thick CRT Glass Refraction & Internal Phosphor Glint Shader

The middle stage is unified into `crtProgram` rendering the curved glass/screen geometry into `sceneFbo` (writing both `sceneColorTex` and hardware `gl_FragDepth` into `sceneDepthTex`).

### 4.1 Layered Thick-Glass Geometry

Instead of two separate translucent meshes (`glassMesh` and `reflectionMesh`) rendered over `screenMesh`, the CRT faceplate is modeled as a physical dielectric slab of refractive index $\eta = 1.52$ and thickness $d(u, v)$:

- **Outer Glass Surface $S_{\text{outer}}(u, v)$:** The rasterized vertex grid at $z_{\text{outer}}(u, v) = d_0 - k_z (u^2 + a_y v^2)$.
- **Inner Phosphor Surface $S_{\text{inner}}(u, v)$:** Located behind the outer glass at thickness $d(u, v) = d_0 \bigl(1 + \beta (u^2 + a_y v^2)\bigr)$, where $d_0 \approx 0.032$ world units (~3.2 cm scaled to the 2.72-unit monitor width) and $\beta \approx 0.18$ models the thicker glass toward the CRT corners (where a physical cylindrical/spherical outer faceplate meets a more curved inner phosphor screen).

### 4.2 Step 1 — View-Ray Refraction Through the Thick Glass

For each fragment on $S_{\text{outer}}$ with world position $\mathbf{P}_{\text{out}}$, surface normal $\mathbf{N}_{\text{out}}$, and incident unit view vector $\mathbf{I} = \text{normalize}(\mathbf{P}_{\text{out}} - \mathbf{E})$:

1. **Refract at Outer Surface ($\text{air} \to \text{glass}$):**
   $$\mathbf{T}_{\text{glass}} = \text{refract}\!\left(\mathbf{I},\; \mathbf{N}_{\text{out}},\; \frac{1.0}{\eta}\right)$$
2. **Trace to Inner Phosphor Surface $S_{\text{inner}}$:**
   - Propagate along $\mathbf{T}_{\text{glass}}$ through the local normal thickness $d(u, v)$:
     $$\Delta\mathbf{P} = \mathbf{T}_{\text{glass}} \cdot \frac{d(u, v)}{\max\!\left(-\mathbf{T}_{\text{glass}} \cdot \mathbf{N}_{\text{out}},\; 0.25\right)}$$
   - Convert the inner intersection $\mathbf{P}_{\text{in}} = \mathbf{P}_{\text{out}} + \Delta\mathbf{P}$ to inner-screen UV coordinates $\mathbf{uv}_{\text{refracted}}$ and map through `imageScale` to sample `screenTex`.
   - **Visual Effect:** Near the center of the screen where $\mathbf{I} \approx -\mathbf{N}_{\text{out}}$, refraction displacement is minimal and text remains razor-sharp. Toward the curved sides and lower-right low-angle corner, the thick glass bends the sightline inward, creating authentic CRT glass thickness and bezel ledge parallax.

### 4.3 Step 2 — Internal Reflection / Halation of Terminal Pixels in the Thick Glass

Terminal glyphs on $S_{\text{inner}}$ are self-luminous phosphors. Light emitted from a bright letter at $\mathbf{P}_{\text{in}}$ travels outward through the thick glass, hits the outer glass-air boundary $S_{\text{outer}}$, and partially reflects back toward $S_{\text{inner}}$, creating a faint secondary ghost/glint image offset along the view/curvature reflection vector and a subtle internal halation halo:

1. **Specular Internal Bounce Offset:**
   - Given the refracted internal ray $\mathbf{T}_{\text{glass}}$, its internal reflection at $S_{\text{outer}}$ mirrors across $\mathbf{N}_{\text{out}}$:
     $$\mathbf{R}_{\text{internal}} = \text{reflect}(\mathbf{T}_{\text{glass}}, -\mathbf{N}_{\text{out}})$$
   - A ray reaching the eye after one internal bounce within the slab of thickness $d(u, v)$ originates from a displaced phosphor coordinate:
     $$\mathbf{uv}_{\text{bounce}} = \mathbf{uv}_{\text{refracted}} + \Delta\mathbf{uv}_{\text{slab}}$$
     where $\Delta\mathbf{uv}_{\text{slab}}$ is proportional to $2 d(u, v)$ projected along the tangential component of $\mathbf{T}_{\text{glass}}$.
2. **Multi-Tap Internal Glint & Halation Sampling:**
   - Sample `screenTex` at $\mathbf{uv}_{\text{bounce}}$ (plus a small 4-tap rotated Poisson/cross kernel scaled by glass thickness $d(u, v)$ to account for phosphor diffuse spread inside the slab).
   - Extract the excess luminance above `terminalBackground` so only lit terminal glyphs/blocks produce internal glass reflection—dark background regions produce zero glint.
   - Weight by internal Fresnel reflectance $F_{\text{internal}}(\cos\theta_t)$ and glass absorption tint (`vec3(0.82, 0.90, 0.96)`), adding a subtle, double-walled CRT glass ghost around high-contrast letters that shifts naturally with viewing angle across the curved screen.

### 4.4 Step 3 — Evaluating the Geometric Light-Source Reflection & Ambient Glass Sheen

Using the CPU-fitted quadratic reflection curve $(\mathbf{c}_0, \mathbf{c}_1, \mathbf{c}_2)$, local widths $(w_0, w_1, w_2)$, and intensities $(I_0, I_1, I_2)$:

1. Evaluate the closest parameter $s \in [0, 1]$ on the quadratic Bezier spine $\mathbf{C}(s) = (1-s)^2 \mathbf{c}_0 + 2(1-s)s\,\mathbf{c}_1 + s^2 \mathbf{c}_2$ to the fragment's outer glass UV.
2. Interpolate the local major/minor reflection radii $w(s)$ and physical intensity $I(s)$ along the spine.
3. Compute the smooth falloff from the reflected sphere contour—using a flat-topped super-Gaussian $\exp(-(r / w(s))^p)$ ($p \approx 2.6$) for the core bulb reflection and a wider Gaussian tail for glass surface scatter (`roughness`).
4. Attenuate by bulk glass grazing Fresnel $F_{\text{outer}}(\mathbf{N}_{\text{out}}, -\mathbf{I})$ and composite in linear light over the refracted + internally glinted phosphor color before applying CRT aperture grille, subtle corner vignette, and bezel edge darkening.

## 5. Retaining the Separable DoF Blur & Mouse Hit-Testing Parity

### 5.1 Two-Pass Separable Gaussian Blur (Unchanged Semantics)

- Pass 2 (`blurHProgram`) and Pass 3 (`blurVProgram`) keep the exact 13-tap Gaussian weights (`±6` taps, `maxBlurPixels = 8.0`), depth validity check (`step(depth, 0.999999)`), and squared proximity falloff:
  $$\text{proximity} = \text{clamp}\!\left(\frac{d_{\text{farthest}} - \|\mathbf{v}_{\text{view}} - \mathbf{f}_{\text{view}}\|}{d_{\text{farthest}} - d_{\text{nearest}}},\; 0.0,\; 1.0\right), \quad r = \text{proximity}^2 \cdot R_{\max}$$
- Because `crtProgram` writes the curved screen's depth into `sceneDepthTex`, both the refracted terminal glyphs, the internal thick-glass letter glint, and the geometric light-source reflection undergo the same coherent optical depth-of-field blur in the near lower-right corner.

### 5.2 Refraction-Aware Pointer Mapping

- When the user clicks or drags on the canvas, `screenCoordinates(event)` intersects the pointer ray with the outer glass paraboloid $S_{\text{outer}}$ using the closed-form quadratic solver, then applies the same single-step Snell refraction offset $\Delta\mathbf{uv}_{\text{refract}}$ used by the shader before converting through `imageScale` to xterm client coordinates.
- This ensures mouse selection and click targets match the refracted glyph positions on screen to sub-pixel accuracy.

## 6. Profiling & Low-End Mobile Optimization Hooks

Because the renderer uses raw WebGL2 without Three.js wrapper state:

1. **Zero-Allocation Frame Loop:**
   - Pre-allocate all uniform vectors, matrices, and the 12–24 sample solver arrays once during `start()`.
   - When the terminal buffer has not changed (`screenNeedsUpdate === false`), skip `drawWarmTerminalImage`, `applyScanlines`, and `gl.texSubImage2D`, running only the 20-microsecond CPU pendulum contour update and the 3 WebGL2 draw calls.
2. **Optional GPU Timer Queries (`EXT_disjoint_timer_query_webgl2`):**
   - Support an opt-in `?profile_gpu=1` (or `options.profileGpu`) instrumentation hook that wraps Pass 1 (CRT + thick glass), Pass 2 (horizontal blur), and Pass 3 (vertical blur) in asynchronous `TIME_ELAPSED_EXT` queries—polling `QUERY_RESULT_AVAILABLE` on subsequent frames without ever calling `gl.finish()`—to report CPU solver vs. GPU pass timings on mobile devices.
